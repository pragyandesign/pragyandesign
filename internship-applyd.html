Applyd

A job application tracker that shows students what needs action today.

Project context

Students and early-career job seekers apply on LinkedIn, Internshala, Naukri, career pages and cold emails. Over time, applications scatter, follow-ups get forgotten, and resume versions get mixed up. I have felt a small version of this myself. When an interview email arrived, it named the company but not the role, the date I applied or what the interview was about, so I had to open the link and dig for the details.

I haven't applied to many companies, so I'm not claiming deep first-hand research. But digging out the job posting every time is frustrating, and it gets worse with every application.

Research: I posted on Reddit to hear from real applicants and got no replies, so I used secondary research from non-India sources, which is a limit. One informal reply arrived after design had begun, suggesting a Notion template. I considered it.

Applyd puts what needs action right at the top of the dashboard, so the user sees it first and can act without hunting for it, and the system works this out for them. In Notion, you would have to build and maintain that yourself.

Design approach

The brief asks for one layout, so I chose a dashboard built on a table. A student needs every application in one view: company, role, stage, date applied, source and resume. A "Needs Action Today" strip sits above it, and red is used only there, so urgency is the loudest thing on screen.

Quick filters answer the brief's own examples:

  • "Follow up on applications pending for more than 10 days"Waiting 10+ days
  • "See all roles where they reached the interview stage"Reached interview
  • "Check which resume version was used for a rejected application"Rejected + the Resume column
  • A visual cue for applications that need action todayThe red cards and tags

What I left out

I kept the product small on purpose. I left out:

  • Kanban boards and timelines. A table shows more applications and details at once.
  • Real resume upload. You can name a resume version, but no file is stored.
  • Email and calendar sync. Everything is added by hand, as the brief asks.
  • A phone app. It is a web app, with a layout that adapts to small screens.
  • Accounts and login.

The Figma screens show quick filter chips only. In the prototype I added a small "refine" row (stage, source, resume, sort) after re-reading the brief's "basic filters or search".

One flow, fully alive

It follows one application from "needs action today" to a finished answer. It covers the brief's four examples: pending 10+ days, reached the interview stage, which resume a rejected application used, and a visual cue for action today.

It also includes small cases that have no static screen: missing required fields, "undo" after a change, and empty states when nothing matches or nothing needs action.

How I used AI

I used Claude for secondary research, low-fi wireframes and prototype code.

I designed the final screens, palette, type and logo in Figma myself. After the first prototype, I tested it as a user and found that the top cards, the add form and the search didn't work as they should. I sent those fixes back, and version 2 covers them.
